\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

\paragraph{Datasets and protocol.}
We evaluate on Visual Genome (VG) \cite{krishna2017visualgenome} and GQA
\cite{hudson2019gqa} under the PredCls protocol used by SDSGG
\cite{chen2024sdsgg}: ground-truth object boxes and labels are given, and the
model predicts only their pairwise predicates. VG contains 35 base predicates
used for training and 15 disjoint novel predicates held out from training. We
also evaluate the base-trained checkpoint on the 24-predicate VG semantic
subset. The SDSGG-compatible GQA setting contains 21 base and 10 novel
predicates selected from categories shared with VG. All training annotations
whose predicate belongs to the target novel split are removed to prevent label
leakage.

\paragraph{Metrics and competitors.}
We report Recall at $K$ ($\mathrm{R}@K$) and class-averaged mean Recall at $K$
($\mathrm{mR}@K$) for $K\in\{20,50,100\}$. The former measures overall triplet
retrieval, whereas the latter gives equal weight to every predicate and is less
dominated by frequent relations. We compare with the category-name CLIP
classifier (CLS) \cite{radford2021clip}, Epic \cite{yu2023epic}, RECODE
\cite{li2023recode}, the direct SDSGG baseline, and APT \cite{luo2026apt} when
their published results use the same split and protocol. A dash in a table
means that the cited method did not report the metric; an empty cell reserves a
required experiment whose value is not yet available.

\paragraph{Implementation details.}
We retain the SDSGG data pipeline and its CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder. The
modality-transfer branch operates on 512-dimensional union-region features and
uses a three-layer MLP followed by a three-layer, eight-head Transformer; at
most 256 positive relation pairs are sampled per training step. The triplet
teacher removes one dominant component. Pointwise alignment and VSP both use
weight 1.0, VSP uses an $\ell_1$ distance, and text-structure loss is fixed to
zero. Novel synthesis uses a pseudo-novel ratio of 0.25 with low-recall
sampling; its reconstruction and KL weights are 1.0 and 0.01, with 2,000-step
warm-up and 6,000-step ramp schedules. The transfer score is fused with SDSGG
logits using weight 1.0. The final submission will report mean and standard
deviation from three seeds together with common-hardware runtime measurements;
the tables retain empty cells where those measurements are pending.

\subsection{Comparison with State of the Art}
\label{sec:exp_comparison}

\input{tables/vg_base_novel}

\paragraph{Visual Genome base and novel splits.}
The full method consistently improves both the direct SDSGG baseline and the
recent APT comparator under the matched Visual Genome protocol. As shown in
Table~\ref{tab:vg_base_novel}, it outperforms SDSGG on every base and novel
$\mathrm{R}@K$ and $\mathrm{mR}@K$ entry, giving average absolute gains of 1.85
and 2.57 percentage points on the two splits, respectively. It also exceeds APT
on all 12 reported entries, with particularly clear novel-predicate gains of
1.8 points at $\mathrm{mR}@20$ and 1.6 points at $\mathrm{mR}@100$. These
matched comparisons support the effectiveness of the proposed transfer branch
without requiring a change to the SDSGG evaluation protocol.

\input{tables/vg_semantic}

\paragraph{Visual Genome semantic subset.}
The improvement extends to predicates selected for richer semantic content.
Table~\ref{tab:vg_semantic} shows that our method raises SDSGG by
2.8/2.4/1.6 points in $\mathrm{R}@20/50/100$ and by 1.8/1.8/0.7 points in
$\mathrm{mR}@20/50/100$. Because the same base-trained checkpoint is evaluated
on this subset, the result provides a harder test of semantic transfer rather
than additional supervised fitting to the 24 target predicates.

\input{tables/gqa_base_novel}

\paragraph{GQA base and novel splits.}
The gain also transfers to the SDSGG-compatible GQA split. Relative to SDSGG,
Table~\ref{tab:gqa_base_novel} reports positive gains on all 12 base/novel
entries, with larger improvements on novel Recall---4.2, 4.6, and 2.7 points at
$K=20,50,100$. We leave APT's matched-protocol row empty rather than combining
its published GQA results with this table, because those results use different
SDSGG reference values and therefore do not establish a controlled numerical
comparison.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}

\input{tables/core_ablation}

\paragraph{Visual structure preservation.}
Visual structure preservation improves every completed base and novel entry in
Table~\ref{tab:core_ablation}. Compared with the variant without VSP, the full
model gains 0.1--1.7 points on the base split and 0.3--3.5 points on the novel
split, averaging 1.28 points across all 12 entries. The larger gains in novel
mean Recall suggest that constraining the pre/post-projection similarity
geometry is especially useful when direct predicate supervision is absent.

\paragraph{Novel synthesis.}
Novel synthesis produces a consistent but non-uniform base--novel trade-off.
Relative to the variant without NS, the full model improves all six novel
entries, including gains of 1.0/1.1/1.6 points in
$\mathrm{mR}@20/50/100$, while decreasing all six base entries by
0.1--1.3 points. We therefore interpret NS as redistributing support toward
unseen predicates rather than uniformly improving every partition; balanced
$\mathrm{F}@K$ reporting in Table~\ref{tab:balanced_metrics} is reserved to
quantify this trade-off consistently.

\input{tables/focused_ablations}

\paragraph{Focused design choices.}
Table~\ref{tab:focused_ablations} predeclares the controlled experiments needed
to isolate dominant-component removal and assess sensitivity to VSP, novel
synthesis, and score fusion. Until those empty cells are measured, the current
evidence supports the complete transfer branch and the two available removal

\subsection{Challenging Evaluation and Analysis}
\label{sec:exp_challenging}

\paragraph{Current challenging settings.}
The VG semantic subset and GQA novel split provide the current tests beyond the
standard VG base/novel comparison. The semantic subset concentrates relations
with richer meanings and is evaluated without retraining, while GQA changes the
image and annotation distribution and withholds its novel predicates from
training. The positive results in Tables~\ref{tab:vg_semantic} and
\ref{tab:gqa_base_novel} therefore show that the observed gain is not confined
to the standard VG partition, while remaining within the paper's PredCls
scope.

\paragraph{Planned stress tests and diagnostics.}
Three analyses are reserved to characterize where the method reaches its limit:
performance stratified by base-predicate frequency, confusion matrices for
near-synonymous predicates, and the correlation between pairwise cosine
similarities before and after modality transfer. The first two test whether
improvements extend to rare and fine-grained relations; the third directly
tests whether VSP preserves the source visual geometry it is designed to
retain. Figure~\ref{fig:challenging_cases} reserves a qualitative comparison of
SDSGG, the two completed removal variants, and the full method; examples will be
sampled uniformly across novel predicates, with a separate panel for the
highest-confidence failure cases.
