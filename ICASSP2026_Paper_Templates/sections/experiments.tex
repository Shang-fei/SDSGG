\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

\textbf{Datasets and metrics.}
We follow the SDSGG PredCls protocol \cite{chen2024sdsgg} on Visual Genome
(VG) \cite{krishna2017visualgenome} and GQA \cite{hudson2019gqa}. Ground-truth
boxes and object labels are supplied, and annotations for target novel
predicates are excluded from training. VG uses 35 base and 15 novel predicates;
we additionally apply the base checkpoint to its 24-predicate semantic subset.
The compatible GQA split contains 21 base and 10 novel predicates. We report
Recall (R@K) and class-averaged mean Recall (mR@K) for
$K\in\{20,50,100\}$.

\textbf{Implementation.}
We retain SDSGG and its CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder. The 512-D transfer branch uses a
three-layer MLP and a three-layer, eight-head Transformer, sampling at most 256
positive pairs per step. We remove one teacher component and set alignment and
VSP weights to 1.0 with $\ell_1$ structure distance; text-structure weight is
0. Novel synthesis uses a 0.25 pseudo-novel ratio, low-recall sampling,
reconstruction/KL weights of 1.0/0.01, and 2k/6k warm-up/ramp. The fusion
weight $\alpha$ is 1.0.

\subsection{Comparison Results}
\label{sec:exp_comparison}

\textbf{Visual Genome.}
Table~\ref{tab:main_comparison} shows that our model improves every matched
SDSGG and APT entry. Relative to SDSGG, average absolute gains are 1.85 points
on base and 2.57 on novel predicates. Against APT, the largest gain is 1.8
points at novel mR@20, while novel mR@100 rises from 32.3 to 33.9. The result
supports the added transfer branch under an unchanged SDSGG protocol.

\input{tables/transfer_analysis}

\textbf{Harder transfer settings.}
The same base-trained checkpoint improves SDSGG on all six VG semantic metrics
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer_analysis}), including +2.8 R@20 and +1.8 mR@20.
On GQA, all 12 base/novel entries also improve; novel R@20/50/100 gains are
4.2/4.6/2.7 points. We reserve APT's GQA row rather than mix its published
numbers with this table because its SDSGG reference values indicate a different
protocol.

\subsection{Ablation and Scope}
\label{sec:exp_ablation}

\input{tables/core_ablation}

\textbf{Component effects.}
VSP improves all 12 completed base/novel metrics over its removal, by 1.28
points on average, with larger gains on novel mean Recall. Novel synthesis
improves all six novel entries over ``w/o NS,'' including +1.0/+1.1/+1.6
mR@20/50/100, but decreases base metrics by 0.1--1.3 points. This exposes a
base--novel trade-off rather than a uniform gain. The independent DCR result,
three-seed variability, sensitivity, and common-hardware efficiency remain
empty in Table~\ref{tab:core_ablation}; no claim relies on these pending
measurements. Evaluation is deliberately limited to PredCls and does not imply
SGCls, SGDet, or transfer to other baselines.
