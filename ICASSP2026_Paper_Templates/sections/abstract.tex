\begin{abstract}
Open-vocabulary scene graph generation must recognize relationships whose
predicate labels are absent from training. However, mapping visual relation
instances to language semantics can distort their fine-grained geometry and
provides no visual support for unseen predicates. We study this problem under
Predicate Classification and propose a relation-preserving modality-transfer
framework built on SDSGG. A frozen triplet-text teacher removes a dominant
shared semantic direction, while a learnable projector combines pointwise
visual--text alignment with preservation of pairwise similarities among source
visual relation features. We further adapt synthesized prompting to generate
relation-conditioned visual features for novel predicates. Experiments under
the SDSGG protocol on Visual Genome and GQA improve the baseline by 2.06
percentage points on average across 30 Recall and mean-Recall entries. On the
matched Visual Genome base and novel splits, our method also outperforms APT on
all 12 reported metrics.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
open-vocabulary scene graph generation, predicate classification, modality
