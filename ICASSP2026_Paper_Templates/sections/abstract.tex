\begin{abstract}
Open-vocabulary scene graph generation must recognize predicates absent from
training. Existing language-guided methods strengthen semantic targets, but
pointwise visual--text transfer may alter visual relation geometry, while novel
predicates lack annotated visual support. We address both limitations under
Predicate Classification with a relation-preserving modality-transfer branch
for SDSGG. A dominant-component-refined triplet teacher supervises a projector
that combines pointwise alignment with preservation of pairwise visual
similarities. A relation-conditioned generator further supplies pseudo visual
features for unseen predicates. On Visual Genome and GQA, our method improves
SDSGG by 2.06 percentage points on average across 30 Recall and mean-Recall
entries. It also exceeds APT on all 12 matched Visual Genome metrics. Ablations
show consistent benefits from visual structure preservation and novel gains
from feature synthesis, with an explicit base--novel trade-off.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
open-vocabulary scene graph generation, predicate classification, modality

\end{keywords}
