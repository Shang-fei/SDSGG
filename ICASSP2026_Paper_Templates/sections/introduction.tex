\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Scene graph generation (SGG) converts an image into objects and their directed
relationships, providing structured input for visual reasoning
\cite{xu2017scenegraph}. Conventional SGG recognizes predicates from a closed
training vocabulary, whereas open-vocabulary SGG (OVSGG) must also identify
unseen relationships. This is difficult because predicates depend jointly on
subject, object, and interaction context, and visually similar pairs can express
fine-grained yet different relations. We focus on Predicate Classification
(PredCls), where ground-truth boxes and object labels are given, to isolate
open-vocabulary relation recognition from detection errors.

Recent OVSGG methods improve semantic matching through richer prompts and
descriptions. Epic and RECODE construct fine-grained cross-modal or composite
cues \cite{yu2023epic,li2023recode}; RAHP, SDSGG, and APT further condition
text representations on entities, regions, scenes, or visual context
\cite{liu2025rahp,chen2024sdsgg,luo2026apt}. These advances strengthen the
semantic target, but they leave the visual-to-text transport itself weakly
constrained. Pointwise alignment can move each visual relation toward its text
target while changing relative similarities among visual instances, thereby
losing distinctions useful for unseen predicates. Moreover, novel predicates
provide no annotated visual instances with which to learn this transport.

Our central hypothesis is that effective open-vocabulary relation transfer
requires both geometry preservation and novel visual support. The first
retains relational organization already present in visual features rather than
forcing it to imitate text geometry. The second populates unseen predicates in
the transfer space without using their visual annotations. Together, these
principles address complementary failure modes of alignment-only transfer.

We realize this hypothesis as a relation-preserving modality-transfer branch
on top of SDSGG. A frozen CLIP triplet teacher \cite{radford2021clip} removes
the dominant shared direction from subject--predicate--object embeddings. A
learnable projector combines pointwise triplet alignment with visual structure
preservation (VSP), which matches pairwise cosine similarities before and after
projection. We further adapt synthesized prompting \cite{wang2023ship} to
generate relation-conditioned pseudo visual features for novel predicates.
At inference, triplet-matching scores complement the original SDSGG logits.

Experiments on Visual Genome and GQA show consistent improvements within this
strict PredCls scope. Across 30 matched Recall and mean-Recall entries, the
full model improves SDSGG by 2.06 percentage points on average and exceeds APT
on all 12 official matched Visual Genome entries. Removing VSP degrades every
completed base/novel metric; novel synthesis improves every novel metric while
revealing a base--novel trade-off. Our contributions are therefore threefold:
(1) we formulate potential geometry distortion and missing novel support as coupled
limitations of relation-level modality transfer; (2) we introduce a unified
framework combining dominant-component-refined triplet semantics, VSP, and
relation-conditioned synthesis; and (3) we establish consistent gains on two
datasets without assuming SGCls, SGDet, or cross-baseline transferability.
