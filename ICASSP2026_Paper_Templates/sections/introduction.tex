\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Scene graph generation (SGG) represents an image as a graph whose nodes denote
objects and whose directed edges describe pairwise relationships
\cite{xu2017scenegraph}. It thereby provides a structured interface between
visual perception and downstream reasoning. Conventional SGG, however,
predicts from a predefined predicate vocabulary and cannot describe
relationships absent from its training annotations. Open-vocabulary SGG
(OVSGG) addresses this limitation by transferring semantic knowledge from
pretrained vision--language models to novel predicates. Such transfer is
particularly difficult for relations: predicates exhibit substantial visual
variation, depend strongly on subject--object context, and often differ through
subtle interaction cues. We study this problem under Predicate Classification
(PredCls), where ground-truth object boxes and labels are provided, thereby
isolating open-vocabulary relation recognition from object-detection errors.

Recent OVSGG methods have primarily strengthened the semantic classifier used
for this transfer. Epic introduces entangled cross-modal prompts for unseen
predicates \cite{yu2023epic}, whereas RECODE composes language-derived visual
cues for zero-shot relation detection \cite{li2023recode}. More recent methods
enrich text representations with subject--object, region, or scene context:
RAHP constructs relation-aware hierarchical prompts \cite{liu2025rahp}, SDSGG
generates scene-specific descriptions and reweights them according to the input
\cite{chen2024sdsgg}, and APT converts frozen semantic features into adaptive,
context-aware prompts \cite{luo2026apt}. These approaches substantially improve
the content and adaptivity of textual relation representations. Nevertheless,
adapting the semantic target alone does not control how visual relation
instances are transported into that space. Pointwise visual--text alignment can
bring each sample closer to its target while altering the relative geometry
among visual instances, potentially discarding distinctions needed for
fine-grained and unseen predicates. ACC recently preserves inter-pair
consistency between teacher and student models for end-to-end OVSGG
\cite{li2025acc}; our focus is complementary: preserving source visual-relation
geometry across an explicit visual-to-text projection in PredCls.

This observation exposes two coupled obstacles. First, a modality-transfer
function must acquire triplet-level semantics without erasing the relational
organization already encoded by visual features; a purely pointwise objective
does not constrain this organization. Second, novel predicates have no
annotated visual instances, leaving a text-derived classifier without direct
support for their possible visual distributions. We therefore posit that
open-vocabulary predicate transfer should preserve the geometry of observed
visual relations while using relation-conditioned synthesis to populate the
missing novel side of the visual space.

Based on this hypothesis, we introduce a relation-preserving modality-transfer
framework on top of SDSGG. A frozen CLIP teacher \cite{radford2021clip} first
encodes subject--predicate--object sentences and removes the dominant shared
direction of the triplet-text bank. A learnable projector then maps CLIP
union-region features into this triplet space using pointwise cosine alignment.
Crucially, a visual structure-preservation loss matches pairwise cosine
similarities before and after projection, regularizing the transfer without a
text-structure loss. To provide visual support for unseen predicates, we
further adapt SHIP \cite{wang2023ship} into a relation-conditioned generator
that reconstructs base relation features and synthesizes pseudo visual features
from novel triplet semantics. At inference, the resulting triplet-matching
scores complement the original SDSGG predicate logits.

Experiments follow the SDSGG PredCls protocol on Visual Genome
\cite{krishna2017visualgenome} and GQA \cite{hudson2019gqa}, evaluating base,
novel, and, where available, semantic predicate partitions with both Recall and
mean Recall. Across the 30 currently reported Ours-versus-SDSGG metric entries,
our full model improves SDSGG by 2.06 percentage points on average. On the
matched Visual Genome base and novel splits, it also exceeds the official APT
results on all 12 reported metrics. Removing visual structure preservation
degrades every corresponding base/novel metric, whereas novel feature
generation consistently improves novel performance while exposing a
base--novel trade-off. These results support geometry-aware modality transfer
without assuming evidence from SGCls, SGDet, or transplantation to other
baselines.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We target potential geometric distortion during visual-to-text
    relation transfer and the absence of visual support for novel predicates
    as two complementary limitations in open-vocabulary predicate
    classification.
    \item We develop an SDSGG-based modality-transfer framework combining a
    dominant-component-refined triplet teacher, pointwise semantic alignment,
    preservation of source visual-relation geometry, and relation-conditioned
    synthesized support for novel predicates.
    \item Within a strictly PredCls scope, we demonstrate consistent gains over
    SDSGG on Visual Genome and GQA and over APT on the matched Visual Genome
    protocol, while exposing the distinct effects of geometry preservation and
    novel feature generation.
\end{itemize}
